After dusk, before dawn

A real flower with a remarkable rhythm

Parijat flowers open in the evening, releasing their fragrance into the night. By morning, many have fallen beneath the tree. Each small white bloom has a bright orange centre—the colors remembered in the old stories.

The plant is known to botanists as Nyctanthes arbor-tristis, and by familiar names including harsingar and shiuli.

The journey through time

A flower carried through the ages

Parijat's story lives in sacred legend and in the nightly rhythm of the tree itself.

  1. 01
    The cosmic beginning

    Born of the churning ocean

    In Hindu sacred lore, Samudra Manthan—the churning of the cosmic ocean—brought wondrous treasures into the world. Among them was the Parijat, a celestial tree whose blossoms were prized by the gods.

  2. 02
    Indra's celestial garden

    A heavenly tree takes root

    Indra, king of the gods, is said to have planted the tree in his heavenly garden. Its fragrant white flowers, with their bright saffron centres, became a symbol of rare beauty.

  3. 03
    Krishna's time on Earth

    A gift, and a story of devotion

    In a popular telling, Krishna brings the Parijat tree to Earth for Satyabhama. Though planted in her courtyard, its branches lean toward Rukmini, whose hands gather the fallen blossoms. Versions vary; the enduring tale speaks of love that cannot be owned.

  4. 04
    A living tradition

    Still blooming after dusk

    Today the real tree, Nyctanthes arbor-tristis, is known as night-flowering jasmine, harsingar, or shiuli. Its flowers are still gathered for worship, remembered in seasonal traditions, and cherished in gardens across South Asia.

These episodes belong to Hindu mythology and folklore; tellings and details differ across traditions.
